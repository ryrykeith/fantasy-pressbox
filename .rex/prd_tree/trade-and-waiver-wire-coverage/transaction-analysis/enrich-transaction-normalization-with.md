description: "FINDINGS FROM THE DJ MOORE PROTOTYPE (2026-10-08) — read before starting\nA one-off trade-grade prompt was built by hand for the week 3 DJ Moore trade in the operator's own league, to see what a good grade actually needs. What it showed:\n\n1. ROSTERS AS OF THE TRADE, NOT TODAY. A grade judges the deal against the rosters it was made with. Today's rosters already include later moves. The prototype used the trade week's raw matchup entries (data/raw/<season>/week-<n>.json, each roster's players/starters) for the \"before\" picture. Record the trade's completion time (status_updated) too: a trade that clears Monday night after the Sunday games leaves that week's points with the old team, and the edition has to say so.\n\n2. SPENT PICKS ARE MOST OF A STARTUP YEAR'S TRADES. Five of the six trades in that league's 2026 data were pick swaps made during the startup draft, using picks consumed when it ended. isFuturePick already drops spent picks, and a transaction left with no moves is already dropped. Keep both, and add a test with a pick-only startup swap so it can never come back as a \"trade\".\n\n3. IDENTIFY TEAMS BY ROSTER ID, NOT NAME. Team names change freely mid-season (user4817 renamed to \"Pick Six Appeal\" between weeks 3 and 4 and broke ranking movement). Carry roster_id through the structured side and resolve names for display only. This must follow whatever the ranking-rename fix settles on.\n\n4. USEFUL FACTS THE PROTOTYPE INCLUDED: each traded player's week-by-week points before and after the trade, and for which team; each side's record and points-for rank; draft capital after the trade (normalizeFutureDraftCapital).\n\n---- original description ----\n\nnormalizeTransactions currently emits { type, week, teams, bid, moves[] } where moves are formatted strings like 'Team A gets Josh Allen (QB, BUF, age 29)'. A grade needs structure, not sentences.\n\nWHAT TO ADD\n- Keep the human-readable strings (the existing incidental use depends on them) but add a structured side: per-transaction, per-team, what was received and what was given up, as player references rather than prose.\n- Attach the acquiring team's positional shape before and after the move: starting slot requirements come from league.startingSlots, and the roster is on the team record. A WR added to a team already starting three good WRs is a different move from the same WR filling a hole.\n- Attach the scoring profile context from the format engine epic, so a TE acquired in a TE-premium league is graded on the right scale.\n- For waiver claims, attach the winning bid AND the acquiring team's remaining FAAB, so the cost can be judged as a share of budget rather than as a raw number.\n\nACCEPTANCE\n- Each transaction carries enough structure for a model to say who won it and why, without the model inventing roster context.\n- Existing consumers of the moves strings keep working unchanged."
